How to Break Up With Someone You Live With: Lease, Bills & Moving-Out Checklist

To break up with someone you live with, prepare a safe place to stay, check your lease and shared bills, then communicate your decision clearly. Agree on a short-term living arrangement, contact relevant housing and financial providers, and record decisions about possessions, children, pets and moving out.Moving out does not automatically end rent or debt obligations. What you owe depends on your contract and local law; get any provider or landlord release in writing. Never put yourself in danger to complete a joint agreement.This guide includes a practical 12-step plan, lease and bills tables, boundary scripts and a printable 30-item moving-out checklist with separate guidance for unsafe situations.

The Last Key on the Table

Their keys had always lived in a blue ceramic bowl by the door. On rainy mornings, the bowl collected coins, grocery receipts, a button from a winter coat, and notes neither could remember writing. The building was old; the stairwell smelled of wet plaster, and one step cried out like a bird when anyone climbed past midnight.

By November, Lena and Tom had stopped asking how the other's day had been. They still remembered the correct medicine for the cat, the cupboard that held the spare batteries and the way the radiator clanked before falling asleep. Their home knew more about them than either was willing to say.

On Thursday, Lena found Tom making soup. The landlord's letter lay beside two warm bowls: would they renew for another year? He was talking about changing the bedroom curtains. She had been rehearsing one sentence all afternoon and still could not find a way to say it without making the kitchen smaller.

“I don't want to continue as partners,” she said. Tom turned off the flame. Downstairs, someone dragged a bicycle over the paving stones. A clock marked the minute with the complete indifference of clocks. She had expected a long argument about why. Instead, Tom looked at the envelope and asked, “What happens to the apartment?”

They did not solve everything that night. Lena stayed at her sister's for two evenings. When she returned, they used separate notebooks. In one they wrote what could be decided: the landlord, electricity, the sofa, the cat's medication, the date of the next rent payment. In the other were things with no easy settlement: how a home could remain standing after the future inside it had ended; why the blue bowl seemed to belong to both and neither.

On moving day, one key remained beside the bowl. Lena wrapped it in newspaper without knowing which room might one day hold it. Outside, sunlight lay on the wet street. That morning she did not feel healed. She felt the peculiar relief of knowing which box to carry first.

Original fictional illustration, not a real couple's testimony. Where there is abuse or intimidation, a shared planning meeting may not be safe.

The practical difficulty of breaking up when living together is that one decision ends the romantic relationship but may leave the lease, bills, possessions and care commitments in place. Separating those decisions is the beginning of a workable plan.

How to Break Up With Someone You Live With: 12 Steps

Start with a safety and housing fallback plan, then end the relationship clearly and document what happens next. This sequence is for situations in which direct discussion is safe. If you fear retaliation, violence or coercion, jump to the safety-first guidance.

  1. Check your safety: identify threats and the support you would need to leave safely.
  2. Arrange a first-night option: consider trusted people, temporary accommodation, accessible transport and pets.
  3. Gather your own documents: lease, deposit receipt, bills, personal identification and financial records you are entitled to access.
  4. Make an emergency budget: estimate upcoming rent, transport, overlap, deposits and minimum loan payments.
  5. Have the breakup conversation: communicate the decision without promising reconciliation to settle the lease.
  6. Set an immediate housing plan: decide where each person can safely sleep and how shared rooms will work.
  7. Contact the landlord or housing adviser: learn the legal options before promising a move-out date.
  8. List all shared bills: identify account holders, payment dates, joint debt and what needs a formal change.
  9. Inventory possessions: distinguish personal belongings, jointly purchased items and disputed property.
  10. Make arrangements for children or pets: focus on their routines, welfare and applicable legal requirements.
  11. Review digital privacy: examine your own accounts, shared services, devices and location sharing safely.
  12. Record the move-out handover: keys, cleaning, meter readings, outstanding payments and future contact.

These steps can overlap. A landlord may need weeks to respond; some situations require moving immediately for safety. There is no universal number of days in which everyone must leave.

Before You Have the Breakup Conversation

A little preparation prevents the first difficult evening from turning into a housing emergency. Identify an affordable, safe place you could stay temporarily, a trusted contact and access to your own essential items. If children or animals depend on you, factor their needs into the first-night plan.

Gather your paperwork

Read the lease or mortgage documents, note every name, the end date and notice provisions, and find deposit records. Write down utility providers, account holders, next payment dates, insurance policies, internet contracts and shared loans. Save copies only of documents you have a right to access; don't secretly enter your partner's private accounts.

Estimate the financial transition

You might need to cover two homes temporarily, or pay transport, storage, a new rental deposit, medical costs or childcare. Write down the next three essential payments. Do not assume a rent obligation disappears simply because a relationship ends.

Choose when to talk

In a non-threatening situation, choose a quiet time outside work and child-care pressure. Consider arranging private space afterward. If there has been intimidation or surveillance, don't assume a face-to-face discussion is the safest choice.

What to Say When You Break Up With Someone You Live With

Be clear about the decision and separate it from the practical negotiation. Avoid announcing the breakup as a threat to win a fight, and avoid offering false hope because you feel anxious about rent.

A straightforward breakup script

“I've thought carefully about us, and I've decided to end our romantic relationship. I know we still share a home, and we can't settle everything tonight. I want to work through the practical details respectfully. Can we start with where we'll each stay for the next couple of days?”

If they ask for another chance

“I hear that you want to try again. I don't want to leave you with an impression that my decision is undecided. We do need to make a separate plan for the home.”

If the discussion becomes overwhelming

“I can't make fair decisions about the lease in the middle of this conversation. Let's make tonight manageable and review the paperwork separately tomorrow.”

Kindness does not require negotiating whether you are allowed to end a relationship. But a script cannot guarantee a safe reaction; use professional safety planning if the other person may threaten or harm you.

Living Together After a Breakup: House Rules That Help

Exes can sometimes remain in the same home temporarily if there is no intimidation, both respect the breakup and they have a realistic route toward separation. It is not a requirement and may be harmful where boundaries are repeatedly violated.

You don't owe sexual contact, shared bed space or romantic rituals because you still pay rent together. A short period of logistics-only communication may be more realistic than strict no contact. Our no-contact guide covers the emotional question separately.

Breaking Up With Someone You Share a Lease With

Leaving an apartment does not necessarily end your tenancy or rent liability. This depends on where you live, what you signed, whether you rent or own, your marital status and any applicable orders. Confirm the legal process with the landlord or qualified local housing adviser rather than treating a private agreement as a lease amendment.

Both names are on the lease

Check whether you are joint tenants, each have separate agreements or have some other arrangement. Ask whether one person can assume a new lease, whether assignment is allowed, whether you can terminate early and whether both will remain liable until a formal release. Request the answer in writing.

Only one name is on the lease

The other occupant may still have rights. Never assume you can immediately evict them, change locks or block access. Laws differ significantly by country and, often, by state or region. Seek advice about tenancy and occupancy rights before acting.

You own the home or share a mortgage

Title, mortgage liability, family rights, equity and living arrangements are separate matters. A buyout or sale can require local legal and financial advice. Avoid signing away ownership rights or changing locks based on an online article.

Citizens Advice explains housing options in England, illustrating how the answer changes between joint tenancy, sole tenancy and ownership. Its details are not universal law.

Email to a landlord

“Hello, we are separating and need to understand the options for our rental at [address]. Could you confirm whether one tenant can remain, the process for ending or assigning the tenancy, applicable notice and fees, deposit arrangements, and how any departing tenant would receive written release from responsibility? Thank you.”

Splitting Bills After a Breakup: Rent, Utilities and Joint Debt

Make one table with the account, both people's names if applicable, payment due date, current balance and who must contact the provider. Separate what you agree between yourselves from what a lender or utility company will legally accept.

AccountWhat to checkNext action
Rent or mortgageContract names and due datesRequest written transfer or release options
Electricity, gas, waterAccount holder and readingsAsk for closing or transfer instructions
Internet and mobileAccount ownership, equipment, penaltiesChoose a lawful termination or transfer date
Loans or joint creditBoth borrowers, balances, autopayContact lender before assuming liability has changed
Insurance and subscriptionsRenewal and linked payment methodsAdjust or cancel with provider as appropriate

The U.S. Consumer Financial Protection Bureau says divorce itself generally doesn't remove a borrower from an existing creditor agreement. For an unmarried breakup or a different country, contracts and applicable law must still be checked individually.

Sample temporary bills agreement

“From [date] through [date] we will each contribute [amount] toward rent while we seek a written agreement with the landlord. [Name] will handle the electricity provider and share the closing statement. This note is a temporary understanding between us, not a release of responsibility by any provider.”

Never withhold essential payments simply to punish an ex. If money is controlled through threats or surveillance, consult a specialist before making account changes.

Security Deposit, Damage and Cleaning When You Move Out

Check who paid the deposit, where it is held, whether a deposit-protection system applies and when the landlord can return it. A partner leaving first may not be entitled to an immediate payment from the landlord, even if you both want to divide it now.

  1. Keep the move-in inspection report, deposit receipt and condition photographs.
  2. Take dated pictures of the property's condition and documented damage without invading privacy.
  3. Record final meter readings and the date they were taken.
  4. Agree which cleaning and repairs are needed, avoiding guesses about what can lawfully be deducted.
  5. Request a written statement of any deductions and how a deposit refund will be handled.
  6. Document keys, access cards and any rented equipment returned.

Do not unilaterally deduct a presumed deposit share from rent. Local law determines when deductions or deposits may be withheld and whether a housing dispute resolution service exists.

How to Divide Furniture, Shared Purchases and Personal Belongings

Make three lists: clearly yours, clearly theirs and genuinely disputed. Record an item's description, receipts or purchase evidence, approximate current value, proposed destination and collection deadline. The person who wants an object most is not necessarily its legal owner, and rules for gifts or purchases differ by jurisdiction and marital status.

Three ways to resolve shared items

  • Keep items each person bought: straightforward when ownership is clear.
  • Agree on a buyout: discuss a fair secondhand amount for a shared item.
  • Sell and divide proceeds: sometimes useful when neither wants an item or a fair buyout can't be agreed.

Do not hide documents, medication, accessibility aids or work equipment to force a concession. If the dispute is significant, a mediator may help in a safe situation, or a lawyer may be necessary where property rights are uncertain.

Who Gets the Dog or Cat When You Split Up?

Pets need consistent food, medicines, safe transport and a stable routine. Decide who can realistically provide care rather than using the animal as leverage for renewed contact. Check adoption or purchase documents, microchip details, veterinary records and pet insurance.

If you propose shared pet care, ask whether it is genuinely practical and safe. A plan that creates constant conflict may be worse for the animal than one settled home. Legal treatment of pet ownership varies: there is no single international pet custody rule.

If abuse is a concern, ask a domestic violence service about animal-safety planning. You should not be forced to contact a threatening partner simply to retrieve your pet without specialist advice.

Breaking Up While Living Together With Kids

Children should not become messengers or judges. Focus on predictable care, school transportation, food, medical needs and clear age-appropriate information about what is known.

A simple explanation

“We are going to live in different homes. You did not cause this, and you don't have to fix it. We both care about you. We'll explain what we know about where you'll sleep and who will take you to school, and you can ask us questions.”

If it is safe and appropriate, write down interim arrangements for school days, contact, expenses and care. Existing custody or child arrangements orders should not be ignored. GOV.UK offers jurisdiction-specific information for child arrangements in England and Wales, with different processes elsewhere.

Where there is domestic abuse or a child-safety concern, mediation or joint planning may be inappropriate. Seek qualified independent support.

Digital Privacy After a Breakup: Passwords, Photos and Location

A move does not automatically undo digital sharing. Review accounts you own, shared subscriptions, family trackers, cloud photo libraries, smart locks, security cameras, stored payment cards and home internet equipment.

  1. From a device you trust, review email recovery information, your own passwords and multi-factor authentication.
  2. Identify which apps have shared calendars, files, photo albums or location access.
  3. Review subscriptions and cards without using access you are not authorized to have.
  4. Consider how smart-home devices and doorbell cameras will be managed lawfully.
  5. Back up documents you are entitled to retain; do not erase or take other people's private information.

Safety warning: if your partner may be monitoring your devices, sudden password or location changes could alert them. Use a safer device and seek technology-safety advice before making visible changes.

Copy-and-Paste Boundary Scripts for Moving Out

Readers of this site search for boundary scripts, but the purpose here is specific: making shared-home separation concrete without reopening the relationship debate.

Separate the breakup from the lease

“My decision about the relationship is final. We still need to make a fair and lawful plan for the apartment. Can we focus this discussion on housing?”

Request important paperwork

“Could you share the latest tenancy agreement and deposit receipt, please? I'd like us to confirm the options with the landlord before either of us commits to a date.”

Ask for logistics-only communication

“I need space from personal conversations while we sort the move. Could we use email for rent, belongings, pets and practical arrangements for now?”

Discuss a disputed sofa

“Rather than arguing about the sofa, let's check who paid for it and whether we can agree on a current value or sell it.”

Confirm handover

“I plan to collect the agreed items on [date]. Let's confirm keys, meter readings, cleaning and outstanding bills in writing, and discuss another safe arrangement if needed.”

For more general copy-and-paste boundary scripts, see the separate communication guide. Do not use these messages instead of a safety plan where threats are present.

When Breaking Up and Moving Out Is Unsafe

When you fear violence, intimidation, stalking, coercion or financial control, you do not have to organize a joint conversation or fair furniture meeting before prioritizing your safety. Leaving an abusive relationship can be a high-risk time, so a personalized plan is more useful than a universal script.

  • Speak confidentially with a domestic abuse advocate or trusted support person using a safe device.
  • Discuss when and how to leave, transport, somewhere to stay and plans for children and pets.
  • Consider essential ID, medication, keys and documents when gathering them does not increase risk.
  • Do not assume it is safe to change passwords or location settings if your devices are being monitored.
  • Get location-specific housing and legal support about access, tenancy and protective orders.

In the U.S., The National Domestic Violence Hotline provides help. See its guidance on living with an abusive partner and preparing to leave. In another country, seek the appropriate local service. If danger is immediate, contact local emergency responders as safely as you can.

Important: A perfect checklist is not more important than being safe. Ordinary advice about negotiation, notice or shared digital access may not apply when someone is threatening you.

After Moving Out: Contact, Recovery and the Next Chapter

Even when you leave, final deposits, bills and keys may require limited communication. Keep it to practical matters where possible, and store a record of what providers actually confirmed. If you co-parent, contact arrangements should focus on children's needs and any binding orders.

Our how to get over a breakup article covers emotional recovery; our no contact after breakup article covers the separate question of communication and boundaries. Here the aim is simpler: make the shared home cease to be a shared unresolved obligation.

Moving out does not require instant healing. You may feel sorrow about the home and relief about the future on the same afternoon. The written checklist cannot answer every emotional question. It can, at least, keep an overdue electricity bill from being one of them.

Breaking Up While Living Together: Frequently Asked Questions

How do you break up with someone you live with and have no money?

Start with safe temporary accommodation, public benefits or housing support where available, a small emergency budget and local tenant advice. Do not assume moving out ends lease or debt obligations.

Can you break up and still live together?

Sometimes temporarily, when the situation is safe, private boundaries are respected and you have a realistic housing plan. A review date and clear rules can help.

What if both names are on the lease?

Contact your landlord or local housing adviser about lawful changes and written release. Moving out may not cancel rent liability.

Who pays the rent after a breakup?

Payment duties depend on the lease and local law. Private agreements may not change what the landlord can demand from tenants who signed.

Can my ex force me to leave if I am not on the lease?

Not necessarily. Occupancy and family rights vary. Get location-specific advice before assuming you must leave immediately or someone can change the locks.

How do we split the rental deposit?

Check the contract, receipt, local deposit rules and how the landlord can release it. Document any agreed contributions or deductions.

Who keeps furniture and shared purchases?

Create an inventory and review ownership evidence. Discuss a buyout, sale or another mutual arrangement, with legal advice for valuable disputes.

Who keeps the dog after a breakup?

Prioritize stable pet care, medical needs and lawful ownership records. Rules differ by location and shared care may not be practical or safe.

Can exes share a bed while still living together?

Only if both freely want that. Separate sleeping arrangements may make boundaries clearer; a shared lease never creates any obligation for physical intimacy.

How long can exes live together after breaking up?

There is no fixed timeline. It depends on finances, safety, housing availability, tenancy law and family responsibilities.

Can I do no contact while we share a lease?

You may need limited logistics-only communication about rent, keys, children or belongings. Emotional no contact and practical responsibility are different.

What if my ex refuses to move out?

Don't forcibly remove someone or change locks without advice on your rights. Contact a housing adviser or lawyer; prioritize safety if there are threats.

Should we tell children before moving?

Explain confirmed changes in simple, age-appropriate terms, avoid blame and follow applicable parenting arrangements. Child safety takes priority.

How do I protect digital privacy after a breakup?

Check your own accounts, recovery methods, subscriptions and location sharing from a safe device. If there may be surveillance, consult a safety specialist before making visible changes.

Can we make our own agreement about bills?

A written summary can help, but does not necessarily alter provider contracts, property rights or legal obligations. Obtain provider confirmation where needed.

How do I ask to be removed from a lease?

Ask the landlord about assignment, termination or a new agreement, fees, notice and a written release. Do not assume a request itself ends the tenancy.
