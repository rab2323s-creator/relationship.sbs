The Call That Looked Real

At 11:47 p.m., Nora finally got the video call she had been asking for. The man on her screen wore a gray sweater. He laughed when her dog barked. The lighting changed as he turned his head. For three weeks, his photograph and messages had been the first thing she checked each morning; now, for the first time, he seemed to occupy the same moment she did.

“See?” he said. “I'm real.”

Two days later, a message arrived about a frozen business account. He needed a short-term transfer to release the funds before they could meet. Nora remembered the face, the laugh and the dog. She almost sent the money.

Then she noticed something she had overlooked: every proof of his identity came from him, while every practical step toward meeting her came with another obstacle. She called her bank before she clicked the payment link.

The important detail wasn't a glitch in the video. It was the demand that an apparently intimate connection should replace independent verification.

In this fictional composite, Nora stopped in time. Many people encounter the same question later: if I saw them, how could they be fake?

What Is an AI Romance Scam?

An AI romance scam is a relationship-based deception in which a fraudster uses AI-generated or manipulated content—such as profile pictures, messages, audio or video—to appear trustworthy while seeking money, access or sensitive information. AI is not necessary for romance fraud, but it can make impersonation faster, more scalable and harder to recognize.

The central fraud remains familiar: build trust, create emotional dependency or urgency, then ask for something costly. The new danger is that a profile photo, thoughtful message, a voice note or even a live-looking video call can be manufactured or manipulated.

Quick answer: A video call, familiar voice or reverse-image-search result cannot independently prove who someone is. The strongest warning signs are pressure to send money, investment instructions, credential requests, contradictory identity details and repeated excuses for not meeting safely. If you've already paid, contact your payment provider immediately.

How AI Romance Scams Work, Step by Step

  1. They find you where relationships start. A dating app, Instagram message or other social account gives the scammer a reason to say hello.
  2. They build a plausible identity. A generated image or borrowed profile, consistent backstory and polished conversations make a stranger feel familiar.
  3. They accelerate closeness. Daily good-morning messages, long calls and talk of a shared future may create trust before ordinary verification is possible.
  4. They supply selective 'proof.' Photos, voice notes, documents or video clips appear to resolve doubt—even though every item is controlled by the same person.
  5. They introduce a barrier or opportunity. A visa problem, medical bill, locked account, shipment, alleged investment or crisis explains why money is needed urgently.
  6. They change the rules. When you hesitate, the request can become an appeal to loyalty, secrecy, guilt or fear. After one payment, another fee may appear.

The FBI's Internet Crime Complaint Center warned in a 2024 public service announcement that criminals have used AI-generated images for romance schemes, generated audio for impersonation and videos as supposed proof that an online contact is real. Its 2025 annual report also describes AI-linked confidence and romance scams. These reports describe reported cases, not a test that can identify any one account with certainty. Read the IC3 warning.

Can a Scammer Fake a Live Video Call?

Yes. A video chat can be deceptive in different ways: prerecorded footage can be presented as live, face-altering software can change a real person's appearance, or AI-generated visuals can create a convincing persona. The quality varies considerably; some are obvious, and others are not.

Why the 'wave at me' test isn't enough

Asking someone to turn their head, wave or say a specific phrase may expose a simple prerecorded clip. But a responsive conversation is not a guarantee of identity. A real human scammer can appear on camera under a false name, and manipulated video can become more responsive over time.

A video call should answer one narrow question—did I interact with an image and voice in real time?—not the more important ones: Is this the claimed person? Is the story true? Is this payment safe?

What about a blurred background, odd blinking or mismatched lips?

Visual artifacts can arise from weak connections, compression, lighting and accessibility tools, not only deepfakes. Conversely, a fake may show no visible glitch. Judge the entire pattern and the requests made of you, not an amateur 'deepfake checklist' of eye movements.

Can a Romance Scammer Clone Someone's Voice?

AI voice cloning can reproduce recognizable elements of speech from available audio. Fraudsters can use a synthetic voice message to reinforce a fictional identity, impersonate a real person or create urgency in a supposed emergency. The FBI specifically lists generated audio and voice impersonation among financial-fraud techniques.

If a caller claims to be someone you already know, end the call and contact that person through an established phone number or another channel you obtained independently. For a new online date, voice familiarity is not equivalent to verified identity. Do not use a voice match as the reason to move money.

12 Signs of a Deepfake Romance Scam

1. The relationship becomes unusually intense before you've met

Affection alone is not fraud. The concern grows when rapid intimacy is coupled with secrecy, financial requests or pressure to stop asking questions.

2. They repeatedly cannot meet in a normal, safe way

Work, distance, disability and privacy are legitimate obstacles. A pattern of shifting excuses alongside a request for money is more concerning.

3. Their video, photos and documents all come through one account

Multiple files can still originate from the same fabricated identity. Independent corroboration matters more than the number of media samples.

4. A 'temporary' money emergency appears

Hospital bills, airfare, customs fees, frozen accounts or a stranded relative are common pretexts. Genuine hardship does not create an obligation to pay an online stranger.

5. They suggest an investment instead of asking for a gift

A supposed romance can become a cryptocurrency or investment scheme. A dashboard showing profits is not evidence that funds can be withdrawn.

6. They insist on crypto, wire transfers, gift cards or payment apps

These methods can be hard to recover, particularly when the recipient is unknown or a payment is authorized under deception.

7. They ask for one-time passwords or security codes

A login, verification or bank code is a credential—not a relationship trust test. Do not share it.

8. They send a link to 'verify' your profile or age

Use the service's official app or site, not a link delivered in a private conversation. A request for card details or identity documents is a red flag.

9. The backstory changes when you ask ordinary questions

Look for repeated contradictions across dates, locations, work and plans rather than a single forgotten detail.

10. They discourage outside opinions

'Our love is private' becomes risky when it is used to isolate you from friends, the dating platform or your bank.

11. They claim only you can solve the crisis

Urgency and guilt narrow your decision-making. Legitimate help is not contingent on keeping a payment secret.

12. They return with a 'recovery expert' after a loss

A person promising to retrieve stolen crypto or payments for an upfront fee may be another scammer. Contact the original provider and recognized authorities.

How to Verify an Online Date Without Trusting a Video Call

There is no single safe 'prove you're real' trick. Use a layered process that protects both people’s privacy instead of demanding passports, face scans or private documents.

  1. Stay on the dating platform until you feel comfortable. Platform reporting and safety features may be harder to use after moving to an unmoderated service.
  2. Check the story, not just the face. Compare basic, voluntarily shared details over time. A reverse-image search may uncover reused images, but a clean result proves nothing.
  3. Keep money completely separate. Never transfer money, buy crypto, pay 'release fees' or lend accounts to someone you know only online.
  4. Use independent contact paths when relevant. If someone claims an institutional or professional identity, contact the organization through the number on its official website. Do not use a number or link they supply as your only source.
  5. Make meeting plans that don't require financial dependence. If you choose to meet, select a public location, arrange your own transport and tell a trusted person. Never send travel funds to arrange the meeting.
  6. Respect refusal without abandoning your boundaries. Someone may decline video for valid reasons. You may likewise decline money requests or end contact without accusing them of a crime.
A safer rule: You do not need to prove someone is a fraudster before deciding not to send them money or personal information.

What to Say When Someone Asks for Money

When they ask for emergency funds: “I don't send money to people I know only online. I hope you can contact family, your bank or local services for help.”

When they offer crypto profits: “I'm not interested in investments introduced through dating. Please don't send me trading links or payment instructions.”

When they say the video call proves they're real: “I appreciate the conversation. A call doesn't change my rule about not sending money or identity documents.”

When they ask for a login code: “I never share security codes. I'll handle any account verification directly in the official app.”

When you want to leave the conversation: “I won't continue this contact. Please don't message me again.”

You are not obliged to debate, negotiate or send one final explanation. If the person threatens you, preserve evidence before blocking and use relevant reporting channels.

I Sent Money to a Romance Scammer. What Now?

Act quickly. Being pressured into a payment can happen to careful people, particularly when a relationship has been built over weeks or months. Recovery is uncertain, but speed can matter.

How you paidFirst action
Credit or debit cardContact the issuing bank or card company, report the fraud and ask about dispute or reversal options.
Bank transfer or wireCall your bank or wire provider immediately; request a recall and fraud investigation.
Payment appReport the transaction through the app and contact any linked card issuer or bank.
Gift cardContact the card issuer; keep the card number and receipt as evidence.
CryptocurrencyContact the exchange or platform used, report the wallet and transaction IDs, and file a cybercrime report. Reversal may not be possible.
  1. Stop sending additional money. Do not pay 'taxes,' account-unlock fees or investigation deposits.
  2. Call the payment provider using an official number. Explain that you believe you were induced to send money through romance fraud; describe exactly what happened.
  3. Save evidence. Keep profile URLs, screenshots, messages, phone numbers, transaction receipts, wallet addresses and dates. Do not download suspicious files.
  4. Secure accounts. Change any exposed passwords, enable multifactor authentication and revoke suspicious sessions.
  5. Report the account and incident. For U.S. readers, use ReportFraud.ftc.gov and IC3.gov; contact police or national cybercrime authorities in your jurisdiction.
  6. Tell someone you trust. A second person can help review next steps and resist pressure from a follow-up impersonator.

The FTC's payment-specific guide explains which company to contact for each transfer type. See What To Do if You Were Scammed. It does not guarantee recovery.

What If I Sent Intimate Photos, My ID, or a Verification Code?

If someone threatens to publish intimate images

Do not pay a blackmailer, send additional images or comply with new demands. Preserve evidence of threats, secure your accounts and report the messages. Adults can explore StopNCII.org, which uses device-generated hashes to help participating platforms detect matching intimate images; it does not guarantee removal everywhere. If anyone depicted is under 18, consult NCMEC's Take It Down. For immediate danger, contact local emergency services.

If you shared a passport, ID or bank details

Contact relevant institutions via official channels, monitor accounts and follow local identity-theft procedures. U.S. readers can find a tailored recovery plan at IdentityTheft.gov. Do not send more documents to someone who promises to 'verify' that your identity was not stolen.

If you shared an SMS or authentication code

Immediately change the affected account's password from the official app, review logged-in devices, turn on stronger multifactor authentication where available and tell the platform or bank if access may have been compromised.

What Not to Do When You Suspect a Scam

  • Don't pay to test the relationship. A small first transfer can lead to escalating demands.
  • Don't send a passport selfie to 'prove trust.' Share identification only through services you have independently verified and actually need.
  • Don't attempt to hack the account or retaliate. This can expose you to additional risks and undermine a formal report.
  • Don't hire strangers who promise guaranteed crypto recovery. Recovery schemes often exploit people who have already lost money.
  • Don't shame yourself or someone else. Practical reporting and account protection are more useful than debating whether the story should have seemed obvious.
  • Don't accuse someone of fraud solely because of a camera glitch, speech difference or refusal to video chat. These can have ordinary explanations.

AI Romance Scams: Frequently Asked Questions

Can a scammer fake a live video call?

Yes. Some scammers use prerecorded clips, filters or AI-generated video in live-looking calls. A video call alone is not reliable proof of identity. Consider the person's broader behavior and never send money as a condition of continuing contact.

Can a romance scammer fake someone's voice?

Yes. AI voice cloning can imitate a person's speech from audio samples. A familiar or convincing voice is not proof that the caller is who they claim to be.

How do you tell if a dating profile is AI-generated?

Look for inconsistent information, unusual requests, borrowed images and resistance to safe verification. Reverse-image search may help but cannot reliably detect every synthetic or new image. A polished profile alone is not evidence of fraud.

Is someone a scammer if they refuse a video call?

No. Privacy, accessibility, safety and personal comfort are legitimate reasons to avoid video calls. Treat persistent inconsistencies and especially financial or credential demands as more meaningful indicators.

Can reverse image search find an AI romance scammer?

Sometimes it identifies reused photos or conflicting identities. A completely AI-generated image may not appear in search results, so a clean result does not authenticate a profile.

Why do romance scammers ask for cryptocurrency or gift cards?

These payments can be difficult to reverse or trace. Unexpected pressure to send cryptocurrency, gift cards, wires or payment-app transfers to an online love interest is a major warning sign.

What should I do if I sent money to an online boyfriend or girlfriend?

Stop further payments, contact the bank or payment provider immediately, request recovery options, save evidence, and report the incident to the platform and relevant agencies. Recovery is uncertain and depends on payment method and timing.

What if a scammer threatens to share my intimate photos?

Do not send additional money or images. Preserve evidence, report and block after collecting needed records, secure accounts, and seek support from appropriate reporting services. For adults, StopNCII.org may help limit distribution on participating platforms.

What is the difference between catfishing and romance fraud?

Catfishing involves misrepresenting identity online, which may happen without a financial motive. Romance fraud uses a supposed emotional relationship to obtain money, sensitive information or other benefits. AI can make both forms of deception more convincing.

Can a scammer use my video call to clone my face or voice?

Saved recordings can potentially provide data for impersonation, although not every recording enables a successful clone. Avoid sharing sensitive details or verification codes during a call and tighten account security if you suspect misuse.

Should I ask the person to wave or hold up fingers during the call?

A spontaneous request may expose some prerecorded videos but is not a dependable deepfake test. Do not treat passing such a challenge as proof, and do not send money based on it.

Where can I report an AI romance scam?

In the United States, report consumer fraud at ReportFraud.ftc.gov and internet crime to IC3.gov. Report the account to the dating or social platform. Elsewhere, contact local police and national cybercrime services.
