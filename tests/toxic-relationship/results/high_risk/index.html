Important: This result is not a diagnosis of abuse and cannot assess your immediate safety. If you feel afraid of a partner’s reaction, you do not need a quiz score—or their agreement—to take that fear seriously.

Patterns that deserve extra attention

  • You are afraid to say no or bring up a concern.
  • Conflict includes threats, intimidation, destroying property, blocking exits, or retaliation.
  • Your phone, location, money, friendships, or movements are monitored or controlled.
  • Warmth and punishment alternate in ways that leave you feeling trapped.

Do not make confrontation the goal

If direct confrontation could increase danger, proving the pattern is not the priority. Safety planning, trusted support, and private access to resources can matter more than getting the other person to admit what is happening.

Practical safety-first steps

  • Tell a trusted person what is happening if it is safe to do so.
  • Keep important documents, medication, money, keys, and emergency contacts accessible where possible.
  • Consider device and account privacy if you think your phone or browsing may be monitored.
  • Use a specialized domestic-violence service to think through a safety plan tailored to your situation.

Support resources

In the U.S., The National Domestic Violence Hotline offers safety-planning information and confidential support. love is respect has guidance on boundaries and relationship safety. If you are elsewhere, use a reputable local domestic-abuse service or emergency service appropriate to your country.
