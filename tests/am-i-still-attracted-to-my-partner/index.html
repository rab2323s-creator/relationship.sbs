How to tell whether you're still attracted to your partner

Thinking “I don't feel attracted to my partner anymore” can be unsettling, especially if you still care about them deeply. Attraction is not always the same as the excitement of the first few months. In a long-term relationship, it can feel more like noticing your partner's humor, wanting to share a small discovery, enjoying their company, or feeling drawn to them in quieter ways.

On the other hand, enjoying a partnership doesn't always mean romantic attraction remains unchanged. You might feel fondness and loyalty while questioning the romantic pull. Neither possibility is something a 12-question quiz can decide for you. The purpose here is to make the question more specific and less frightening.

What this attraction quiz measures—and what it doesn't

Every answer contributes to one of four descriptive patterns. The result counts which experiences you chose most often, shows the close alternatives, and gives you examples taken directly from your answers. It does not measure the percentage of physical attraction you have, diagnose a psychological condition, or predict whether your relationship should continue.

1. Attraction that is still present

For some people, the initial rush becomes a steadier sense of interest and warmth. They still notice qualities they admire, enjoy ordinary affection, and feel curious about their partner's experiences, even when every encounter is not exciting. Stable attraction need not look like an early crush.

2. Attraction hidden under routine and stress

Work, chores, caring responsibilities, sleep loss, and familiar habits can crowd out the attention needed to notice an existing connection. This pattern is about the circumstances in which your feelings become more or less noticeable. See our guide to the mental load in relationships for practical context.

3. Emotional distance affecting attraction

Attraction does not happen outside a relationship's emotional history. A disagreement you never resolved, a sense of not being heard, or recurring disappointments can make even affectionate contact feel complicated. A useful next step may be emotional repair rather than trying to manufacture excitement. Read about emotional safety between partners.

4. A more persistent shift in attraction

Sometimes you still appreciate your partner's kindness and enjoy spending time together, but the romantic feeling seems consistently different, including during calm or happy periods. This pattern invites reflection and honest, compassionate communication. It does not mean the quiz has discovered the future of your relationship or that anyone is at fault.

How attraction changes in long-term relationships

Imagine two people who have lived together for several years. They work, manage appointments, and share chores. One starts thinking that the spark is gone. Yet on a relaxed weekend they still find themselves laughing, talking, and appreciating each other. That experience is different from a couple who have stopped trusting one another after repeated hurts. Both may describe “lost attraction,” but the context suggests different conversations.

It's also possible to have a good weekend and still feel that your attraction has changed. A single date, a quiet night, or a one-time moment of closeness cannot establish whether attraction is present or absent. Look for patterns across time rather than treating one moment as a verdict.

Not attracted to your partner anymore: questions worth asking

Instead of asking only whether the spark is gone, consider when you feel differently. Do you notice a shift when you're rested? Is the relationship emotionally safe? Do you feel free to choose affection without pressure? Are you confusing the end of early infatuation with the end of all attraction? Can you point to qualities you still find compelling in your partner today, rather than only remembering the past?

If you're mainly wondering about desire for sexual activity, that is a related but distinct question. Our Why Don't I Want Sex With My Partner? quiz explores stress, wellbeing and pressure around desire specifically. This page is focused on attraction to your partner as a person.

What to do when attraction feels different

Start with observation, not a deadline

Notice the circumstances in which you feel warm, indifferent, curious or distant. Don't set a goal of forcing yourself to be attracted; that tends to turn a feeling into a test you must pass.

Talk about experiences rather than issuing a verdict

For instance: “I've noticed I sometimes feel less connected lately, and I'd like us to understand what's changed.” That's more useful than announcing a diagnosis about the relationship. If there is a specific hurt, discuss that directly. When attraction genuinely feels different over time, it is also okay to be truthful without making promises about how feelings will change.

Protect choice and boundaries

No result requires you to kiss, touch or have sex with someone. Consent and autonomy matter independently of how long a relationship has lasted. If you're feeling pressured, consult our boundaries versus control guide; when safety is an issue, prioritize support over scoring.

Physical attraction, emotional intimacy and compatibility are different

You can feel emotionally safe without having constant physical attraction, and you can feel attraction without having long-term compatibility. Questions about losing attraction

Is it normal to lose some attraction in a long-term relationship?

Attraction can fluctuate and the intensity of an early relationship can change. This does not alone determine whether a relationship is healthy or lasting. What matters is your pattern, your comfort, and whether you can communicate respectfully.

Can I love my partner but not feel attracted to them?

Yes. Caring for someone, feeling emotionally close, and experiencing romantic or physical attraction are overlapping but different experiences. This quiz helps you reflect on context, not declare which feeling is correct.

Does losing the spark mean we should break up?

No quiz can answer that. Consider your broader relationship, emotional safety, mutual respect, and your own values. A change in attraction is information to understand, not an automatic instruction to leave or stay.

Can stress affect attraction to a partner?

Stress and exhaustion can change the attention and energy you bring to a relationship. But they are not the only explanation. If your attraction feels different even in calmer periods, give that information equal respect.

What if I care about my partner but feel more like friends?

Friendship and companionship are valuable, and people experience romance differently. You can notice this distinction without judging yourself or your partner. If the mismatch brings distress, compassionate discussion may help.

What if my partner wants affection when I don't?

You can decline any physical or sexual contact. Repeated pressure after a refusal is important to address separately from any attraction profile. If you feel unsafe, reach out to trusted support appropriate to your circumstances.
