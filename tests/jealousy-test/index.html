Jealousy Test: Am I Too Jealous in My Relationship?

Think about your current or most recent relationship. Answer from the repeated pattern—not one bad day. This 12-question test looks at whether jealousy stays proportionate or turns into suspicion, reassurance loops, monitoring, or control.

What this Jealousy Test measures

Jealousy is not one thing. Classic research on the Multidimensional Jealousy Scale separated it into cognitive, emotional, and behavioral components. This test uses that insight without reproducing the validated scale, then adds a fourth everyday mechanism: the reassurance-and-comparison loop.

Suspicion · 28%

How quickly ambiguity becomes a story about betrayal, rejection, or hidden motives.

Emotional reactivity · 18%

How strongly jealousy changes your mood, body, attention, or ability to think clearly.

Reassurance & comparison · 24%

How much safety depends on proof, being chosen, or repeated reassurance.

Monitoring & control · 30%

Whether jealousy becomes checking, tracking, testing, restricting, or pressuring autonomy.

The weighting is intentional: feeling jealous is not the same as controlling behavior. Monitoring and unsupported suspicion count more than emotional discomfort alone.

The story: one “who’s that?” turned into a whole evening of detective work

Composite story: fictionalized from common patterns, not a real client.

Mara saw a new name appear in Leo’s notifications while they were watching a movie. “Nina.” She asked who it was. “Someone from work,” he said.

That answer should have been enough information for one moment. Instead, Mara’s mind immediately generated three more questions: Why hadn’t he mentioned Nina before? Why was she texting at night? Why did Leo turn the phone over after answering?

By the time Leo fell asleep, Mara had checked who from his company followed him on Instagram, searched Nina’s name, compared photos, and reread Leo’s last few messages looking for changes in tone.

The next morning Leo explained that Nina was coordinating a deadline. Mara felt relief for about twenty minutes. Then a new question arrived: “But why was she comfortable texting him that late?”

The important part was not that Mara felt jealous. The important part was that each answer briefly reduced anxiety but created the need for another piece of proof.

Why the score separates feelings from behavior

The original multidimensional jealousy research found that cognitive, emotional, and behavioral jealousy were related but distinct. Emotional jealousy can exist even in loving relationships; suspicious thinking and behavioral monitoring tell a different story. citeturn807268search0

How to interpret your Jealousy Pattern Score

  • 0–19: jealousy looks mostly proportionate.
  • 20–39: some jealousy loops are active.
  • 40–59: jealousy is starting to run the relationship.
  • 60–79: strong jealousy pattern.
  • 80–100: high-control jealousy pattern.

This is an original educational score, not a diagnosis or probability. The result also shows your two strongest dimensions so the same total can have a different meaning depending on what is driving it.

When jealousy crosses into monitoring or control

Jealousy can be understandable and still lead to harmful behavior. Research on online mate-retention tactics found that jealousy/surveillance behaviors were associated with partner aggression, while recent reviews also describe links between jealousy, controlling behavior, and intimate partner violence. citeturn807268search7turn807268search5

If jealousy is leading to tracking, threats, isolation, humiliation, forced password access, stalking, or aggression, the behavior deserves attention regardless of the score.

Jealousy Test FAQ

What does a jealousy test measure?

This test looks at suspicious thinking, emotional reactivity, reassurance/comparison loops, and monitoring or control. It is educational, not diagnostic.

Is jealousy always unhealthy?

No. Jealousy is a common emotion. The more useful question is whether it matches the evidence and what behavior follows from it.

How is this different from retroactive jealousy?

Retroactive jealousy focuses on a partner’s past. This test covers broader present-day jealousy across ambiguity, comparison, reassurance, and control.

Can betrayal make me more jealous?

Yes. Previous betrayal can sensitize you to threat. Repair works better when it uses clear, time-limited agreements instead of permanent surveillance.

Does a high score mean I am toxic?

No. A score cannot label a person. It can show which patterns deserve closer attention, especially unsupported suspicion and controlling behavior.
