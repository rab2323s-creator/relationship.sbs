When “I miss them” is not the same question as “Do I love them?”

Nora knew exactly how many minutes it had been since his last message because she had checked without meaning to. Twenty-three. Then thirty-one. Then forty-six.

When he replied, relief arrived before happiness. That detail bothered her.

She loved the way he listened. She knew what made him nervous. She wanted good things for him even when those things had nothing to do with her. But she also noticed that the relationship felt most powerful when she was afraid of losing it.

So the question changed from “Am I in love?” to “How much of what I call love is care—and how much is attachment urgency, infatuation, or the future I have imagined?”

What this Am I in Love quiz measures

A major psychological framework describes love through intimacy, passion, and commitment. A 2026 scoping review of 27 studies found these components still meaningfully related to relationship satisfaction, seriousness, and sexual satisfaction. This quiz uses that research as background, then adds two distinctions people searching “love or attachment?” specifically need: reality-based knowing and attachment urgency.

Grounded Love

Care, respect, goodwill, and wanting the person—not only the feeling they produce in you.

Real-Person Knowing

How much your feelings are based on the person as they actually are, including limits and imperfections.

Secure Choice

Whether connection feels chosen rather than required to keep you emotionally stable.

Attachment Urgency

Fear of loss, reassurance hunger, difficulty tolerating distance, and relief-driven closeness.

Idealization & Infatuation

Fantasy, intensity, novelty, projection, and how much possibility outruns actual knowledge.

Love vs attachment vs infatuation

Grounded love can include passion, but it survives ordinary days and increasing knowledge of the real person. Attachment urgency is often loudest around separation, uncertainty, or the need for reassurance. Infatuation can feel vivid and sincere while still relying heavily on novelty, projection, and imagined possibility.

These are not mutually exclusive boxes. You can love someone and also have anxious attachment. You can feel infatuated while real love is beginning to grow. The value of the result is in the composition, not a yes/no label.

How the 12-question scoring works

Each answer contributes to all five dimensions rather than adding one simplistic “love point.” The engine normalizes every dimension to 0–100 and calculates a Grounded Love Score from Grounded Love, Real-Person Knowing, and Secure Choice. It then interprets that score alongside Attachment Urgency and Idealization.

Your final profile is one of five patterns: Grounded Love, Growing Love, Love With Attachment Anxiety, Idealized Infatuation, or Attachment-Led Bond. You also see your two strongest dimensions and the full five-dimension breakdown.

The score is an original educational reflection score—not a probability that you are “really in love,” and not a clinical threshold.

Am I in Love or Just Attached? FAQ

How do I know if I am in love or just attached?

Look for what remains when urgency settles. Grounded love usually includes knowing the real person, care for their wellbeing, respect, choice, and the ability to stay connected without losing yourself.

Can attachment feel like love?

Yes. Attachment can create longing, comfort, separation distress, and strong relief when a person returns. Those feelings can coexist with love.

What is the difference between love and infatuation?

Infatuation is often more dependent on novelty, intensity, idealization, and limited information. Love can include passion, but tends to become more reality-based as you know the whole person.

Can I love someone and still have anxious attachment?

Yes. Genuine love and attachment anxiety can coexist. One result profile is specifically designed for strong care combined with strong fear-of-loss or reassurance signals.

Is calm love less real than intense love?

No. Intensity can come from passion, novelty, uncertainty, anxiety, or longing. Calm does not prove love either, but safety and predictability do not make love less real.

Is this a scientifically validated love test?

No. It is an original research-informed educational quiz. These 12 items, weights, profiles, and cutoffs have not been psychometrically validated.
