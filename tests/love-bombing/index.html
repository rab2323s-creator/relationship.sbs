Love Bombing Test: Am I Being Love Bombed?

What this Love Bombing Test actually measures

Most love bombing quizzes can become misleading if they treat romance itself as the problem. Frequent texts, flowers, long dates, strong chemistry, or early excitement can happen in healthy relationships. This test gives more weight to what happens when you stop matching the intensity.

Your result is built from four dimensions. Each dimension contains three scenario-based questions, and each answer is scored from 0 to 4. The overall Love Bombing Pattern Score is then normalized to 0–100. The two strongest dimensions are shown separately so two people with the same total can receive different explanations.

Accelerated attachment · 20%

How quickly certainty, idealization, gifts, and future plans outrun how well you actually know each other.

Boundary pressure · 30%

What happens when you slow down, say no, protect privacy, or choose a pace they do not prefer.

Access & dependency · 22%

Whether closeness becomes constant availability, emotional responsibility, or pressure to shrink your outside life.

Control & reactivity · 28%

Whether warmth turns into monitoring, withdrawal, punishment, or a hot-cold cycle after disagreement or limits.

That weighting is deliberate. Fast attachment alone cannot produce one of the highest results. A person can be intensely interested and still respect your autonomy. Pressure and control are more decision-useful than the number of compliments you receive.

The night the romance changed because she asked for one quiet weekend

Composite story: This is fictionalized from common relationship patterns, not a real client or identifiable person.

For three weeks, Elise had never wondered where she stood. Noah texted before she woke up. He remembered tiny details. He sent lunch to her office after she mentioned skipping breakfast. On their fourth date, he said he had never felt so understood by anyone.

Her last relationship had been vague and inconsistent, so the certainty felt almost medicinal. No guessing. No “what are we?” conversation. Noah talked about a concert six months away, a winter trip, the neighborhood he wanted to live in one day. Elise knew it was fast, but fast did not feel dangerous. It felt clear.

Then her sister came to town.

Elise texted Noah: “I’m going to keep this weekend for her. I’ll call you Sunday night.”

He replied with a heart. Then another message: “Both days?” Then: “I thought we were past the stage where we needed space from each other.” By dinner, he had stopped responding. At midnight he sent, “I guess I misread how serious this is.”

Nothing dramatic had happened. He had not threatened her. He had not called her names. But the relationship suddenly had a price for moving at her speed.

That is the distinction this test is built around. The first three weeks tell you how someone behaves when closeness is easy. A small boundary tells you how they behave when your autonomy interrupts the fantasy.

Love bombing vs genuine affection: the friction test

Cleveland Clinic describes love bombing as excessive attention or affection that can become manipulative, while also noting that the behavior may be intentional or unintentional. The most useful signal is not one romantic act; it is whether the person respects limits and your separate life.

Why the questions focus on behavior instead of labels

“Is this person a narcissist?” is a tempting question because a label seems to promise certainty. It is also a weak basis for a self-test. Love-bombing-like behavior does not diagnose narcissistic personality disorder, and the research base on love bombing itself is still limited.

A 2017 study of 484 college students reported associations between love-bombing behaviors, narcissistic tendencies, insecure attachment, lower self-esteem, and heavier text/media use in romantic relationships. It was an early, correlational study in a narrow population, so this test does not convert those findings into a diagnostic checklist.

Instead, the questions ask what a person actually does with your pace, “no,” privacy, time, support system, and disagreement. Those behaviors are easier to observe and more useful for decision-making than trying to infer a personality disorder from romantic intensity.

Why a safety signal is separate from the total score

One serious behavior can matter even if most of the relationship feels loving. That is why the result separately flags answers involving coercion, monitoring, isolation, fear, or punitive reactions instead of averaging them away.

Research and public-safety guidance on coercive control emphasize patterns that restrict autonomy and create fear or dependence. Australia’s eSafety Commissioner, for example, warns that constant checking, pressure for digital access, and love-bombing behavior can become part of technology-facilitated coercive control.

A safety flag does not mean this quiz has diagnosed abuse. It means the specific behavior deserves attention independent of whether the overall Love Bombing Pattern Score is 35 or 85.

How to interpret your Love Bombing Pattern Score

The score is an original educational model created for this test. It is not a validated clinical instrument, and the score is not a probability that someone is manipulating you.

  • 0–19: intensity looks mostly calibrated in the behaviors asked here.
  • 20–39: fast intensity; slow down and watch what happens when you set pace.
  • 40–54: mixed pattern; pressure or dependency is becoming more visible.
  • 55–79: strong love-bombing-like pattern across meaningful dimensions.
  • 80–100: high-pressure/control pattern; autonomy and safety deserve priority.

Two people can both score 58 and need different interpretations. One may score high because of boundary pressure and control after disagreement. Another may show accelerated attachment plus dependency pressure. That is why your result displays the two strongest dimensions instead of relying on the total alone.

Love Bombing Test FAQ

What is a love bombing test?

A love bombing test is a self-reflection tool that looks for patterns such as unusually fast attachment, pressure around boundaries, demands for access or dependency, and negative reactions when the relationship slows down. It cannot prove intent or diagnose abuse.

How can I tell love bombing from genuine affection?

The clearest difference is often what happens when you introduce friction. Genuine affection can tolerate your pace, privacy, friendships, and boundaries. A more concerning pattern becomes pressuring, punitive, controlling, or unstable when you say no or slow down.

Is a high Love Bombing Pattern Score a diagnosis?

No. The 0–100 score is an original weighted reflection score for this questionnaire. It is not a clinical cutoff, probability, diagnosis, or proof of another person’s intent.

Can someone move fast without love bombing me?

Yes. Strong mutual attraction can be intense. This test intentionally weights boundary pressure and controlling reactions more heavily than fast romance alone.

Is love bombing always narcissistic?

No. Love-bombing-like behavior does not diagnose narcissistic personality disorder. One limited study found associations with narcissistic tendencies and insecure attachment, but that evidence cannot diagnose an individual.

What if my result is high but I do not feel afraid?

A high score still deserves a slower pace and a closer look at your two strongest dimensions. Fear is not required for a relationship pattern to be unhealthy. At the same time, the test cannot tell you what decision to make.

What if my score is low but one answer involved monitoring or coercion?

Take the specific behavior seriously. The test intentionally shows a separate safety signal because a serious boundary violation should not disappear inside an average.

What should I do after the test?

Use the result as a structured reality check. Keep or restore your normal support system, slow major commitments if the pattern is unclear, state one concrete boundary, and watch what happens next. If you fear retaliation or escalation, seek private real-world support rather than confronting someone because of a score.

Research notes & sources

Love bombing is a popular relationship term, not a formal mental-health diagnosis, and the research base is much smaller than the internet conversation around it. This test therefore uses original scenario questions and an original scoring model. It is informed by research and safety literature on early relationship intensity, attachment, coercion, boundaries, and control, but it is not presented as a validated psychological instrument.
