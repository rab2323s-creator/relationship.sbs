Is My Friendship One-Sided? The Short Answer

A friendship may be one-sided when you consistently provide more initiative, emotional support and repair than you receive, and your needs or boundaries are repeatedly overlooked. However, one-sided effort is a pattern—not proof from one unanswered text, one canceled plan or a difficult month. The useful question is whether both friends can show care, take responsibility and respond to reasonable requests over time.

If you keep searching is my friendship one sided, one sided friendship quiz or am I the only one trying in my friendship, the problem may not be how frequently you text. It may be that you always arrange the contact, hold the painful conversations and rearrange your life, while the other person never seems to notice your limits. This quiz is designed specifically for platonic friendships, not romantic partners.

What the 15-Question Friendship Reciprocity Quiz Measures

Healthy friendships can be different in closeness, frequency and practical capacity. A friend might send one message a month and still show up in a difficult situation. Another might message daily but dismiss your needs whenever they are inconvenient. That is why the quiz separates contact frequency from responsiveness and mutual care.

Seven Signs a Friendship Might Feel One-Sided

  1. You initiate nearly everything. Invitations, check-ins and repair attempts rarely come from the other direction.
  2. You are available in their difficult moments, but feel invisible in yours. A one-time imbalance is different from a longstanding pattern.
  3. Your good news rarely gets space. Their stories are welcome, while yours are cut short or overlooked.
  4. Plans depend on your follow-through. A friendly response rarely turns into a concrete invitation or rescheduled date.
  5. Your no is treated as a betrayal. Declining a favor triggers pressure, guilt or punishment.
  6. You apologize for raising concerns. Conflict repair becomes your job even when you were hurt.
  7. You feel smaller or more exhausted after contact. That feeling warrants attention, especially when it repeats across settings.

A single sign is not a verdict. Look at whether the friendship has been this way for a long time, whether circumstances explain a temporary imbalance and whether honest communication changes anything.

One-Sided Friendship or Just a Hard Season?

Friendship is not a ledger that must balance every Friday. Someone caring for a relative, facing illness, beginning a demanding job or grieving a loss may need more support than they can offer for a while. That can be a normal part of closeness, particularly when they acknowledge the imbalance, respect your limits and care about your wellbeing even if they cannot always act on it.

A recurring pattern looks different: your friend expects you to be available indefinitely, does not ask how you are, takes your help for granted and gets angry when you set a limit. The contrast lies not in exactly equal favors but in the possibility of mutual respect.

Try looking back over the last six to eight weeks if the friendship is active, or a longer period if you rarely meet. Ask what was happening for each person and whether the same roles have existed beyond the current season. The window is a practical suggestion—not an evidence-based cutoff.

What Research Says About Reciprocity, Support and Friendship

Friendship research distinguishes supportive behavior from how supported someone actually feels. In a 2023 study of 132 young-adult friend pairs, researchers observed discussions of personal problems and examined how verbal responses related to each friend's perception of support. Negative engagement was associated with the listening partner feeling less supported, while friendship quality was associated with support perceptions. This was a particular young-adult sample; it does not establish a universal scoring threshold for friendship.

A 2014 study using hypothetical scenarios with 772 participants found that reciprocity expectations and gratitude were reported more strongly toward friends than toward siblings. That is consistent with the importance of mutuality in friendship, but it does not mean keeping exact accounts is healthy.

In research on friendship dissolution among emerging adults, participants described different ways of managing difficult friendships, including distancing, limiting certain aspects of a friendship and ending it. Responses varied by the situation. That helps explain why a result here offers choices rather than a command to cut someone off.

Research on friendship challenges and social competence also identifies support situations, competing interests and interpersonal transgressions as important situations to navigate. This quiz draws on broad themes of friendship support and conflict, but its questions and scores have not been validated by those researchers.

What to Say When You Feel Like the Only One Trying

You do not need to accuse a friend of being “toxic” to make a clear request. Start with one observable situation, your experience and a realistic alternative.

When you always initiate: “I enjoy spending time with you, but I've been the one suggesting our last few plans. Could you choose the next time we meet?”

When you are always the listener: “I want to be there for you. I'd also like us to make room for what's going on in my life.”

When favors feel expected: “I can't help this weekend. I can let you know when I do have capacity, but I don't want to promise what I can't give.”

When a boundary triggers guilt: “I hear that you're disappointed. I still need to keep that limit, and I'd like us to discuss it without blame.”

These examples are conversation starters, not scripts guaranteed to change another person. For more examples, see healthy boundary scripts for friends and ways to express your needs clearly.

What to Do If Your Friendship Is One-Sided

  1. Name the actual pattern. “I start the plans and check-ins” is clearer than “You never care.”
  2. Check context. Is this a temporary period of grief, illness or heavy responsibilities—or a longstanding expectation that you will do everything?
  3. Make one manageable request. Ask for a shared plan, a turn to talk, or respect for a no.
  4. Notice the response over time. Good intentions matter, but so does observable change. Avoid inventing secret tests or withdrawing as punishment.
  5. Choose the level of closeness you can sustain. You may repair, reduce contact, change expectations or end the friendship. You do not need a numerical result to grant yourself permission to set boundaries.

Friendship Reciprocity vs. a One-Sided Romantic Relationship

This is a friendship-only quiz. It asks about invitations, mutual care, social belonging, boundaries and emotional effort between friends. It intentionally does not assess exclusivity, intimacy, romantic promises, attachment in dating or couples' future plans. If your concern is about a boyfriend, girlfriend, spouse or dating partner, use our separate One-Sided Relationship Test instead.

Friendship can also take many forms: a close best friend, an online friendship, an old friend you see twice a year, or a circle of people with different levels of closeness. The goal is not to rank your friends. The result should help you ask what kind of connection is possible and what you need.

How the 15-Question Friendship Score Works

Each of the 15 situations offers four response options worth 4, 3, 1 or 0 editorial mutuality points. Three questions make up each of five dimensions, shown as 0–100. The weighted summary uses initiative 22%, support 24%, being seen 17%, boundaries 20% and emotional energy and repair 17%. It describes the patterns in your selected answers; it is not an estimate of how much your friend loves you.

A response describing intimidation, punitive behavior, breaches of confidence or attempts to control you triggers a boundaries-first profile even if other scores are high. No other positive answer should cancel out a safety concern. There is no research-validated cutoff or ability to diagnose a friend's character, and a person who never answered this quiz cannot be assessed by it.

One-Sided Friendship Quiz: Frequently Asked Questions

How do I know if my friendship is one-sided?

Look for a repeated pattern in which you are usually the person initiating plans, offering support, asking about the other person's life and repairing disagreements, without comparable interest or respect in return. Do not judge the friendship by one busy week or a strict 50/50 count.

What is a one-sided friendship?

A one-sided friendship is a platonic friendship that feels persistently unequal in effort, support, interest or consideration. It is not a medical diagnosis or a precise category; people can contribute differently at different times.

What are signs you are the only one trying in a friendship?

Possible signs include always initiating contact, repeatedly rescheduling on your own, receiving little support when you need it, being pressured to do favors, or feeling drained and afraid to express a need. Context and your friend's actual response to a conversation matter.

Does it mean a friendship is one-sided if I always text first?

Not by itself. Some friends are less comfortable with messaging, travel frequently or show care by making plans or offering help. Consider the wider pattern of responsiveness, reliability and respect.

Should I stop texting first to test my friend?

You can stop carrying an unsustainable share of planning, but using silence as a trap rarely creates clarity. A straightforward request for more initiative or a less demanding contact rhythm can provide better information when it is safe.

Can a friendship be uneven without being unhealthy?

Yes. Illness, grief, caregiving and demanding work can temporarily change what someone can give. Mutual acknowledgment and respect for limits matter more than exactly equal effort in every week.

How do I tell a friend I feel like I am doing all the work?

Use a specific recent example and a manageable request: 'I've initiated our last several meetups, and I'd like us both to suggest plans sometimes. Could you choose the next time?' Listen to their circumstances and notice whether anything changes.

What if my friend only reaches out when they need something?

Consider whether it is a short difficult period or a repeated pattern. You can decline favors, ask for a more mutual friendship and adjust how much you give. You do not have to keep saying yes to prove that you care.

Should I end a one-sided friendship?

Not automatically. You may choose to repair, reduce contact, change expectations or end it depending on the pattern, safety and willingness to respond to boundaries. A quiz cannot make the decision for you.

Is this the same as a one-sided romantic relationship test?

No. This quiz focuses on platonic friendship: invitations, mutual support, social inclusion, boundaries and the strain of being the permanent organizer. It does not measure dating, sexual intimacy or romantic commitment.

Can I take this friendship quiz about a best friend or a long-distance friend?

Yes. Think about the pattern in that specific friendship rather than comparing your contact frequency to an ideal. For long-distance friendships, weigh realistic contact, meaningful support and follow-through instead of how often you meet in person.

Is the friendship reciprocity score scientifically validated?

No. The 15 questions, weights and result profiles are original educational tools. They are inspired by broad friendship research, not taken from or validated as a diagnostic scale, and do not predict whether a friendship will last.
