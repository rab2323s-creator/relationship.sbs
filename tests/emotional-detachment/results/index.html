Still Emotionally Engaged

Your answers suggest that your partner still matters to you in an emotionally immediate way. You still want to share, understand, repair, and include them in your future. Stress may be present, but the relationship has not become emotionally irrelevant.

Strained but Reachable

Your answers suggest that emotional connection has become harder to access, yet part of you still wants repair, closeness, or meaningful change. This is different from indifference: the relationship still matters, but the emotional cost of staying engaged has gone up.

Protective Withdrawal

Your answers suggest that distance may be helping you stop getting hurt rather than proving you no longer care. Repeated disappointment, circular conflict, unmet needs, or emotional overload can teach a person to invest less because investment keeps costing too much.

Relationship-Specific Detachment

Your answers suggest that the disconnection is concentrated in this relationship rather than across your whole emotional life. You may still care about your partner as a person while feeling less curiosity, less desire for repair, and more relief when distance increases.

Broader Emotional Numbness Signal

Your answers suggest that the relationship may not be the only place where feeling has become muted. When friends, hobbies, pleasure, motivation, good news, or connection in general also feel flatter, relationship detachment becomes harder to interpret in isolation.
