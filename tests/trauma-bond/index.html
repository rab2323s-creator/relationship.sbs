Trauma bonding can develop when a person who repeatedly hurts or controls someone also offers affection, apologies, or relief. Abuse, coercive control, or an imbalance of power matters here: a hot-and-cold relationship by itself does not establish a trauma bond.

Signs of a Trauma Bond

These patterns can be reasons to seek support and reflect on your safety, especially when they happen together:

  • Harm, intimidation, or control is repeatedly followed by affection, promises, or reconciliation.
  • You minimize what happened or blame yourself to preserve the connection.
  • Boundaries are punished, your independence is restricted, or you feel increasingly isolated.
  • Leaving feels difficult even though the relationship repeatedly makes you feel afraid or unsafe.

Am I Trauma Bonded?

This trauma bonding quiz asks about patterns over time rather than one argument or one intense moment. Answer the 15 scenarios based on what happens most often. Your result is an educational reflection profile, not proof of trauma bonding or a clinical assessment.

Is It Love or a Trauma Bond?

Healthy love allows safety, autonomy, and repair that leads to sustained change. A trauma bond involves attachment within repeated harm and an unequal or coercive dynamic, often reinforced by periods of relief. Intensity, longing, and inconsistent texting alone do not prove abuse.

Trauma Bond vs. Push-Pull vs. Healthy Love

Push-pull dynamics describe recurring closeness and distance; they can occur without abuse. Trauma bonding involves additional harm and power imbalance. FAQ

What is a trauma bond in a relationship?

A trauma bond is an attachment formed in a harmful or abusive relationship, often involving power imbalance and repeated distress followed by relief or affection. The pattern can make leaving difficult.

What is intermittent reinforcement in relationships?

It means affection, attention, or reassurance appears unpredictably—sometimes intensely—then disappears. Unpredictability can strengthen attachment because your nervous system starts chasing the next “good” moment.

Is trauma bonding the same as love?

No. Healthy love supports safety, autonomy, and lasting repair. Trauma bonding involves attachment within repeated harm or control, sometimes reinforced by affection or relief after distress.

Is this trauma bond test a diagnosis?

No. This is an educational self-reflection quiz about patterns. It does not diagnose a person or mental health condition and does not replace professional support.

Why can’t I leave a toxic relationship?

Many people struggle to leave when the relationship includes powerful relief moments after distress. This can create hope resets and emotional cravings. Support and clear boundaries can help you regain clarity.
