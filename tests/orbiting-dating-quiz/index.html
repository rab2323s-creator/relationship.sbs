The person who disappeared from the conversation but not from the screen

Nora stopped expecting his name in her messages on a Thursday.

By Sunday she had almost accepted the silence. He had not answered the last question she sent. He had not suggested another date. He had not said he was busy, confused, scared, or finished.

But every evening, there he was.

First on her Story viewer list. Then a like on a photo. Two days later, a fire emoji under a picture of the city at night.

Each appearance was tiny enough to dismiss and large enough to restart the question.

If he is done, why is he still here?

That is the psychological power of orbiting. The connection can be absent where relationships actually happen—conversation, plans, accountability, clarity—and still remain visible where almost no risk is required.

The central rule of this test: digital attention is not scored as romantic intent. It becomes an orbiting signal when visibility stays high while direct effort and follow-through stay low.

What this Orbiting in Dating Quiz measures

Digital Visibility

Story views, likes, reactions, profile activity, and how consistently the person remains visible online.

Direct Contact Gap

The gap between seeing their name online and receiving meaningful messages, conversations, or honest communication.

Follow-Through Gap

Whether attention leads to calls, plans, rescheduling, meeting, or sustained relationship movement.

Intermittent Re-entry

“Hey stranger,” memes, emojis, late-night pings, and other small returns that briefly reopen the connection.

Ambiguity Impact

How much their digital presence changes your hope, checking, posting, rumination, or ability to move on.

Orbiting vs Breadcrumbing vs Genuine Reconnection

PatternWhat you usually seeWhat is missing—or present
OrbitingStory views, likes, passive reactions, repeated digital presenceMeaningful direct contact and relationship movement are missing
BreadcrumbingOccasional texts, flirting, memes, “thinking of you,” small bursts of attentionConsistency and progression remain weak
Genuine reconnectionDirect communication plus online attentionThere is follow-through: conversation, accountability, plans, rescheduling, or sustained effort
Passive presenceOccasional views or likesNo strong pattern; the activity may be ordinary social-media behavior

This distinction matters because a Story view is cheap evidence. A concrete plan that survives inconvenience is stronger evidence.

Why orbiting can keep you stuck even when nothing is happening

Research does not prove that every person who watches an ex online is “orbiting,” and it cannot tell us why another person clicked your Story. What it does show is that post-breakup social-media exposure and observation can matter psychologically.

A study of 464 people found that Facebook surveillance of an ex-partner was associated with greater breakup distress, negative feelings, sexual desire and longing, and lower personal growth. More recent 2026 research across four studies found both active and passive observation of ex-partners on social media were associated with poorer aspects of breakup recovery, including distress, negative affect, and jealousy.

That is why this quiz includes an Ambiguity Impact dimension but does not use your distress as proof of another person's motive. Your reaction tells us what the digital pattern is doing to you—not what is secretly happening in their mind.

  • Marshall (2026) — social-media observation, breakup distress, negative affect and jealousy

How the Orbiting Pattern Score works

Every answer contributes to a primary and, where useful, a secondary dimension. The overall score gives the most weight to the pattern that defines orbiting: high digital visibility paired with a large direct-contact and follow-through gap.

Intermittent re-entry helps distinguish classic orbiting from breadcrumbing crossover. Ambiguity Impact is reported because it matters to your wellbeing, but it receives less weight in the core behavior score so that your anxiety does not become evidence against the other person.

The final profile also checks the shape of the scores. Someone who watches every Story but also calls, makes plans, and follows through should not receive the same result as someone who never directly reconnects.

This is an original educational scoring model, not a clinical measure and not a way to infer another person's private intentions.

15 signs that matter more than one Story view

  • They stopped direct contact but remain consistently visible online.
  • They watch or like without starting meaningful conversations.
  • They react to content, but your response does not develop into dialogue.
  • They resurface after silence without addressing the silence.
  • They send low-risk pings but avoid concrete plans.
  • They ignore direct messages while engaging with public content.
  • They keep the connection psychologically present but relationally inactive.
  • They avoid answering what they want.
  • They show curiosity about your life without offering clarity about theirs.
  • The pattern repeats after ghosting, a breakup, or a dating fade-out.

None of these proves motive. The quiz is designed to rank the pattern by behavior rather than invent a hidden explanation.

What to do if someone is orbiting you

The most useful question is often not “Why are they doing it?” but “What does this pattern allow me to know?”

  1. Separate visibility from investment. A view is not a plan.
  2. Look for direction. Does contact move toward conversation, repair, or meeting?
  3. Ask once if clarity would genuinely help. You do not need to interrogate every notification.
  4. Stop using passive attention as a substitute for direct evidence.
  5. Change access if the pattern keeps you stuck. Muting, unfollowing, restricting, or blocking can be boundaries rather than punishments.

Orbiting in Dating FAQ

What is orbiting in dating?

Orbiting usually means someone has reduced or stopped meaningful direct contact while continuing to remain visible around your social media through views, likes, reactions, or profile activity.

How do I know if someone is orbiting me?

Look for a repeated combination of high digital visibility and low direct relationship effort. One Story view by itself is weak evidence.

Is watching all my Stories orbiting?

Not automatically. Habitual Story viewing is common. The behavior becomes more orbit-like when meaningful contact has disappeared while digital presence remains unusually consistent.

What is the difference between orbiting and breadcrumbing?

Orbiting is usually passive digital presence. Breadcrumbing includes intermittent direct contact—small texts, flirting, or pings—without enough consistency or progression.

Does orbiting mean my ex wants me back?

No. A Story view cannot reveal motive. Genuine reconnection is better judged through direct communication, accountability, plans, and sustained follow-through.

Why does orbiting make me overthink?

It keeps the person visible while the relationship remains ambiguous. Social-media exposure to ex-partners has been associated in research with greater breakup distress and longing.

Should I block someone who is orbiting me?

You do not have to. But if repeated digital exposure keeps reopening hope, distress, or checking behavior, reducing access can be a reasonable boundary.

Is this quiz scientifically validated?

No. It is an original research-informed educational quiz. The questions, weights, score bands, and result profiles have not been psychometrically validated.
