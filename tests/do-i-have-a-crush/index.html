Do I Have a Crush or Do I Just Like Them as a Friend?

You may have a crush when your feelings toward a specific person include a wish for romantic closeness, imagined dating, or a distinctive attraction that goes beyond how you experience ordinary friendship. Enjoying someone's company, admiring their talents or feeling happy when they text does not automatically mean romance. Nor does an absence of dramatic butterflies prove there is no attraction.

This free Do I Have a Crush? Quiz examines your own interest, not whether they like you back. The questions are meant for an early-stage attraction, including a possible crush on a friend. They are deliberately different from our Does He Like Me? Quiz, which looks at someone else's observable behavior, and Am I in Love or Just Attached?, which asks about deeper patterns.

Quick answer:

A crush is a developing, person-specific interest that may include romantic hopes or attraction. A friendship can be equally meaningful without a desire to date. If you are unsure, consider what kind of closeness you actually want—not only whether they make you feel noticed.

The Bookshop Window: A Story About Feelings Without a Label

Each morning the tram stopped in front of the flower shop at precisely seven forty-three, and each morning Emilia promised herself she would not look toward the window of the little bookshop opposite. Yet the books were stacked there like houses in a quiet town, and behind them stood Elias, arranging the display with the concentration of someone repairing a delicate clock.

For two years he had been her friend. He knew she disliked cinnamon. She knew he called his grandmother on Sundays. They had argued about novels and helped each other carry groceries through the winter snow. Nothing about it had needed a name.

Then, one afternoon, he mentioned that he might move to another city. Emilia was surprised by the peculiar sadness of seeing an empty space where he usually stood. Was it friendship, she wondered, or something that had been growing so slowly she had mistaken it for the ordinary passage of days?

Her sister said she must be in love. Her friend said she was merely afraid of change. Emilia wrote both verdicts on scraps of paper and pushed them beneath the sugar bowl, where they were quickly forgotten. She did not want someone else's explanation. She wanted to know what she would choose if he stayed.

That evening they walked beside the river. Elias spoke about the possible move, about the rent and the weather and a book he had lost. She listened as the city lights settled into the water. She wanted him to be happy, even somewhere else; she also wondered whether she wanted to ask him on a date before he went. The wishes did not cancel each other. They simply made the question more real.

Nothing was decided beside the river. They went home separately. The next morning Emilia looked across the street again, not because she needed a sign that he liked her, but because she had finally noticed a wish of her own.

9 Signs You Might Have a Crush

  1. You look forward to one-to-one time with this person in a way that feels different from ordinary hangouts.
  2. You are curious about their values, small habits and real personality—not only how attractive they look.
  3. Imagining a mutual date feels appealing rather than simply flattering.
  4. You sometimes picture affectionate or romantic moments, even if you do not want physical contact.
  5. You notice their arrival with a special excitement or anticipation.
  6. You wonder what you would say if the chance to express interest arose.
  7. You want to share news or private moments specifically with them.
  8. You care about their choices and wellbeing even when you receive no validation.
  9. When you imagine remaining just friends, you notice a genuine romantic possibility you might miss.

None of these alone proves a crush, and not everyone experiences them. Friendship and admiration can explain many individual signs. Look for the quality and consistency of the pattern.

Crush vs Friendship: How to Tell the Difference

Platonic closeness can be emotionally deep. A friend may be the first person you call with good news, someone you find physically beautiful or someone whose opinion matters enormously. The distinguishing question is not “How much do I care?” but “What kind of connection do I want?”

Do I Have Feelings for My Friend?

A friendship can slowly acquire a new possibility. You may begin to wonder about a date after years of enjoying conversation, or you may simply be frightened of losing someone you love platonically. Don't use jealousy or the intensity of a friendship as the only evidence: those feelings can have many meanings.

Consider an ordinary scene. If your friend suggested an explicitly romantic date, would you genuinely want to explore it, feel uncomfortable, or need time? If you could keep their friendship for years without dating, would that feel mostly right or like a possibility left unexplored?

Respect matters either way. Do not treat a friend's friendliness as consent or a promise. A low-pressure conversation might be appropriate when the friendship feels safe, but you can also choose not to act. Do I Like Them, or Do I Just Like the Attention?

It's normal to enjoy being admired. That pleasure is not a character flaw. But a crush directed toward a real person is different from being excited mainly by the possibility of receiving a flattering message.

Ask: would I still want to hear their stories if no one else knew we talked? Am I interested in their values and ordinary struggles, or only how they make me feel about myself? If someone else offered equal attention, would my romantic curiosity move immediately to them?

Mixed answers are common. You might enjoy attention and genuinely like someone. The aim is not to shame yourself but to avoid mistaking a wish for validation for knowledge of another person. Can a Crush Exist Without Butterflies or Physical Attraction?

Yes. People experience attraction differently, and romantic interest does not require a dramatic physical sensation. Some people are drawn toward shared emotional intimacy or a hoped-for partnership without much physical attraction; others experience aesthetic or physical attraction without wanting a romantic relationship.

Butterflies may reflect excitement, nervousness or uncertainty, and are not a scientifically reliable yes-or-no indicator. Consider your wishes and how you relate to this person rather than trying to meet a stereotype about how a crush should feel.

You also do not owe a romantic explanation for caring deeply. Being aromantic or on the aromantic spectrum can involve experiences that differ from the conventional crush narrative. This self-reflection is not designed to assign orientation labels.

Do I Have a Crush If I Keep Thinking About Their Texts?

Anticipating a reply can come from warmth, attraction, uncertainty or the social habit of waiting for a notification. Re-reading a message once is not proof of a crush; repeatedly analyzing each punctuation mark cannot reveal someone's private intentions.

Instead, notice what you want if the messaging goes well. Are you hoping for an actual date and time together, or do you mainly enjoy being reassured? Your interest looks warm, friendly or appreciative without a strong wish to date. A close bond does not need a romantic label to count.

Emotional Curiosity

Someone has your attention, but you are not yet sure whether the feeling is romantic. Curiosity is a valid place to begin.

Emerging Romantic Attraction

You may be starting to hope for a different kind of closeness. Go slowly enough to discover the actual person rather than the fantasy.

A Clear Romantic Crush

Several answers consistently point to wanting romantic time together. That reflects your feelings, not evidence the other person shares them.

Mixed or Unresolved Feelings

One part of the connection feels romantic while another feels friendly or focused on attention. You are allowed to remain unsure without forcing a definite label.

What Should You Do If You Think You Have a Crush?

  1. Notice the feeling without rushing: it can be interesting to learn something new about yourself.
  2. Learn who they actually are: ask open questions and notice values and kindness rather than assuming a fantasy is the future.
  3. Remember reciprocity is separate: you may like them whether or not they like you back.
  4. Use a low-pressure invitation if appropriate: “I've enjoyed talking with you. Would you want to get coffee sometime, as a date?”
  5. Respect the answer: a no is not a problem to solve. If you are friends, consider what boundaries allow the friendship to remain respectful.
  6. Give uncertainty time: you do not owe a confession because a quiz gave a percentage.

Do I Have a Crush? Quiz — Frequently Asked Questions

Do I have a crush on them or just like them as a friend?

Ask whether you want specifically romantic time or a date, not only whether you care about them. Deep friendship, admiration and attraction can feel close without being identical.

What are signs you have a crush?

You may look forward to seeing one person in particular, imagine a date, feel a special attraction and want to know their real personality. No single sign proves romance.

Can you have a crush on a friend?

Yes. A friendship can include or develop romantic interest. You can also be close, affectionate and protective of a friend without wanting a date.

Is it a crush or do I just like the attention?

Consider whether you would still want to know this individual without public recognition or flattery. Both feelings can coexist, so be gentle about uncertainty.

Does thinking about someone all day mean I have a crush?

Not necessarily. Thoughts can arise from curiosity, novelty, uncertainty, social anxiety or romance. What kind of relationship you want is more informative.

Can a crush be platonic?

Some people use crush informally for intense admiration or a friend crush. Romantic crushes specifically involve some romantic wish; language varies across people.

Can I have a crush without wanting to kiss them?

Yes. Attraction is varied, and some people experience romantic wishes without wanting physical affection. The quiz does not assign orientation labels.

What if I have never had a crush before?

There is no deadline or required experience. Some people rarely experience romantic attraction, and others are unsure what they feel.

What does my crush percentage mean?

The percentage describes how your responses align with this quiz's five editorial areas. It is not a probability that you are in love or that they like you back.

Is this crush test scientifically validated?

No. It uses original scenarios and transparent scoring for educational self-reflection, not a validated psychological instrument.

What if I get the mixed-feelings result?

Your answers may differ across romantic hopes, friendship, curiosity and enjoying attention. You can wait, observe and make your own decision.

Should I tell my friend I have a crush?

You may choose a respectful, low-pressure conversation when appropriate, while accepting that the other person may not share the feeling. A quiz result is not an obligation to confess.

How long does a crush last?

There is no universal clock. The feeling may fade, deepen or shift toward friendship as you learn more about each other.

Does this quiz know if they like me back?

No. It only summarizes what you report about yourself. Another person's feelings cannot be deduced from your score.
