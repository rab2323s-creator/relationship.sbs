Protective Distance

Fear of Intimacy Test — result profile

Your answers suggest that distance may do more than give you breathing room—it may help you feel emotionally safe and in control. Depending on someone, receiving care, or staying present after vulnerable moments can feel risky enough that privacy and self-reliance become your default protection.

What this pattern can look like

  • You often solve problems alone even when support is available.
  • Needing another person may feel more dangerous than loneliness.
  • Reasonable closeness can sometimes register as pressure.
  • Withdrawal may feel easier than repair.

What to do next

  1. Experiment with receiving one small form of help without immediately repaying it.
  2. Use explicit boundaries to protect autonomy instead of emotional cutoff.
  3. If the pattern repeatedly blocks relationships you value, consider qualified therapy focused on the underlying fear.
Do not overread the label

This profile describes the pattern selected in your answers. It is not the original Fear-of-Intimacy Scale, a clinical diagnosis, proof of an attachment style, or evidence about another person's motives.
