Closeness–Distance Conflict

Fear of Intimacy Test — result profile

Your answers suggest an approach–avoid pattern. Part of you may want deep connection, reassurance, and intimacy while another part becomes uncomfortable once the relationship actually offers them. That can create confusing shifts: warmth followed by silence, disclosure followed by regret, or desire followed by sudden fault-finding.

What this pattern can look like

  • You may feel closest to someone and then need to cool the relationship down.
  • Safe availability can feel less exciting or more exposing than uncertainty.
  • You may want reassurance while disliking that you need it.
  • Closeness itself can trigger doubt even when nothing clearly went wrong.

What to do next

  1. Track what happens immediately before the urge to pull away.
  2. Replace unexplained withdrawal with a clear return time.
  3. Avoid making major relationship decisions at the peak of closeness-triggered panic when there is no safety issue.
Do not overread the label

This profile describes the pattern selected in your answers. It is not the original Fear-of-Intimacy Scale, a clinical diagnosis, proof of an attachment style, or evidence about another person's motives.
