Am I Sabotaging My Relationship? What This Quiz Measures

This self-sabotage test is about what you do when connection feels uncertain, not whether your partner loves you or whether a relationship will survive. It uses five descriptive dimensions: defensiveness, trust testing, protective withdrawal, conflict escalation and repair follow-through. Each question presents an ordinary relationship situation with four possible responses.

Choose the option closest to what you tend to do, not the one that sounds most impressive. Results describe your selected responses and their possible implications; they do not reveal unconscious motives or give a diagnosis.

Self-Sabotage Is Not the Same as Protecting Yourself

Sometimes leaving, becoming guarded, or refusing a conversation is a reasonable response to a partner's repeated lies, manipulation, coercive control, or threats. This quiz cannot assess relationship safety. A score must never be used to persuade someone to stay with a partner who harms or intimidates them.

For a complete distinction, see self-sabotaging relationships: 12 signs and how to stop, which includes a comparison of protective boundaries and harmful patterns.

How the 18-Question Quiz Works

Each selected answer is coded from 0 to 3 to reflect how strongly that specific scenario represents a self-protective response that might undermine connection. Each dimension shows the proportion of its possible points selected. Higher bars describe which responses you chose; they are not estimates of how likely you are to lose a partner or how “toxic” you are.

The questions and scoring are original to relationship.sbs and have not been psychometrically validated. They are not the published 12-item Relationship Sabotage Scale developed by Peel and Caltabiano in 2021. Their research identified defensiveness, trust difficulty and limited relationship skills; this educational quiz also includes withdrawal, escalation and repair to make reflection actionable.

What Your Results Can Help You Practice

A high defensiveness bar suggests practicing listening before explaining your intent. A high trust-testing bar suggests asking directly rather than checking or setting traps. Protective withdrawal points toward clear timeouts and return plans; conflict escalation points toward staying with one issue; and repair follow-through points toward behavior change after an apology.

You can make progress on one specific habit while still feeling worried. Your results should prompt a conversation with yourself or a trusted professional, not settle questions about blame.

Research and Editorial Limits

Read the 2021 BMC Psychology paper on the Relationship Sabotage Scale and Peel's 2025 paper on reciprocal relationships among attachment insecurity, stress and defensive strategies. These sources inform the context, but neither validates this site's original quiz.

Self-Sabotage Quiz: Frequently Asked Questions

Am I a bad partner if a bar is high?

No. A high bar only describes choices you selected in hypothetical scenarios. It does not assess character, clinical conditions or your entire relationship.

Can I take this quiz if I'm single?

Yes. Answer based on how you typically react in dating or past relationships. If a scenario does not fit, choose the closest response and treat the result cautiously.

Is this the validated Relationship Sabotage Scale?

No. This is an original educational questionnaire with five descriptive dimensions. It has no established clinical cutoffs or validation studies.

Does the quiz tell me if I should stay or leave?

No. It does not evaluate safety, compatibility, another person's behavior or abuse. Never use it as a reason to ignore a serious red flag.
