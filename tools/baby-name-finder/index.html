THE THOUGHTFUL GUIDE

Choosing a name is a conversation, not a competition.

A name can connect a child to family, language and culture. These practical steps can help you decide together.

01

Begin with what matters

Do you love a sound, a family connection or a particular meaning? Share those priorities before comparing lists. It is easier to agree on a direction than on a name right away.

02

Consider how it travels

Say each name aloud in the languages your family uses. Ask how others might pronounce or spell it, and check whether the name works comfortably with your surname.

03

Give each other room

Make shortlists independently, then look for overlaps. A name one partner dislikes need not become an argument; explore what the other partner loves about it.

What do baby name meanings and origins actually tell you?

Name meanings are usually shorthand for etymology: the word, language or older name from which a modern name developed. One name can have several plausible histories, especially when it is used across languages or shares a spelling with another name. A meaning is not a personality prediction, and cultures sometimes interpret the same name differently.

This finder uses 1,000 distinct names selected from US Social Security baby-name records for 2020, with etymological glosses and roots derived from English Wiktionary entries. The name count is the count of names in this tool, not the number of names registered in any country. Each card's US usage count refers to births registered under that spelling and sex classification in 2020; it is not a popularity measure for the United Kingdom, Germany, France or Europe as a whole.

Meaning filters are intentionally conservative. A name is shown in the “nature” or “love” group when its recorded gloss contains matching concepts, not because its style simply feels natural or romantic. For names with uncertain or competing explanations, check the linked etymology before making a final decision.

How to agree on a baby name as a couple

Try a first pass with no discussion: each parent saves around ten names. Then share a shortlist, identify the names you both selected, and talk about why those work. If there is no overlap, compare qualities—short versus long, familiar versus uncommon, traditional versus modern—rather than trying to win support for a single favorite.

You can also revisit your thoughts about parenthood and explore other relationship guides. Choosing a name is one of many meaningful family decisions you can make as a team.

COMMON QUESTIONS

A few things worth knowing.

Is this baby name finder free?

Yes. You can explore the names, save favorites and share a shortlist without creating an account or paying a fee.

Can I use it with my spouse or partner?

Yes. Save your favorite names, copy a shortlist link and send it to your partner. When they open your link, they can save their own picks and see which names overlap.

Are the name meanings always definitive?

No. Etymological explanations may differ by language, source and naming tradition. Treat the glosses as a starting point, and verify a final choice using the cited dictionary source and speakers familiar with the language.

Does US popularity tell me what is popular in Europe?

No. The birth counts and ranks in this tool use US Social Security data from 2020 only. We do not imply that a name has the same usage level in any European country.

Where are my saved names stored?

Your favorites are saved locally in your browser when storage is available. A share link contains only the selected names in its address. Anyone who receives the link can read those names. We do not store a shared shortlist on a server.
