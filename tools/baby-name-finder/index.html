THE THOUGHTFUL GUIDE

How to Find the Perfect Baby Name

A name can connect a child to family, language and culture. These practical steps can help you decide together.

01

Begin with what matters

Do you love a sound, a family connection or a particular meaning? Share those priorities before comparing lists. It is easier to agree on a direction than on a name right away.

02

Consider how it travels

Say each name aloud in the languages your family uses. Ask how others might pronounce or spell it, and check whether the name works comfortably with your surname.

03

Give each other room

Make shortlists independently, then look for overlaps. A name one partner dislikes need not become an argument; explore what the other partner loves about it.

Search Baby Names by Letter, Gender and Popularity

Start with the search box to find a name or a word in a documented meaning. Choose Girls, Boys or Both to narrow the list, then open More filters to browse baby names by first letter, length or popularity in US birth records. Use “Most used” to explore familiar names, or “Less used” to find names with lower recorded US counts. A lower count is not proof that a name is rare in every region or language.

What do baby name meanings and origins actually tell you?

Name meanings are usually shorthand for etymology: the word, language or older name from which a modern name developed. One name can have several plausible histories, especially when it is used across languages or shares a spelling with another name. A meaning is not a personality prediction, and cultures sometimes interpret the same name differently.

This finder includes 20,000 distinct recorded baby-name spellings. The original 1,000-name set is preserved, and 19,000 additional names were selected from 2025 US Social Security records. Not every name has a verified etymology: those without a documented source are clearly labeled and are excluded from meaning and origin filters. Meanings shown here come from source-attributed English Wiktionary-derived records and still require individual editorial review. The name count is the count of names in this tool, not the number of names registered in any country. Each card's US usage count refers to births registered under that spelling and the recorded sex category with the higher count in 2025 (or a labeled 2020 fallback). Country comparisons show verified 2025 top-ten ranks for England and Wales, France, and Germany only. These rankings are not Europe-wide; German lists group some spelling variants, unlike the exact-spelling lists used elsewhere.

Meaning filters are intentionally conservative. A name is shown in the “nature” or “love” group when its recorded gloss matches explicitly defined meaning concepts, not because its style simply feels natural or romantic. For names with uncertain or competing explanations, independently verify the linked etymology before making a final decision.

Choose Baby Names Together: Compare Your Favorites

Try a first pass with no discussion: each parent saves around ten names. Then share a shortlist, identify the names you both selected, and talk about why those work. If there is no overlap, compare qualities—short versus long, familiar versus uncommon, traditional versus modern—rather than trying to win support for a single favorite.

You can also revisit your thoughts about parenthood and explore other relationship guides. Choosing a name is one of many meaningful family decisions you can make as a team.

COMMON QUESTIONS

Baby Name Finder FAQs

Is this baby name finder free?

Yes. You can explore the names, save favorites and share a shortlist without creating an account or paying a fee.

Can I search baby names by starting letter?

Yes. Open More filters and choose the first letter you want. You can combine this with the name search and the girl, boy or gender-neutral options.

Can I use it with my spouse or partner?

Yes. Save your favorite names, copy a shortlist link and send it to your partner. When they open your link, they can save their own picks and see which names overlap.

Do all 20,000 names have a verified meaning?

No. All 20,000 names are linked to published US birth records, but only a subset has source-attributed meaning and language-root entries. When meaning or origin is not documented, the tool says so instead of guessing. Meaning and origin filters search only documented entries.

Are the name meanings always definitive?

No. Etymological explanations may differ by language, source and naming tradition. Treat the glosses as a starting point, and verify a final choice using the cited dictionary source and speakers familiar with the language.

Does US popularity tell me what is popular in Europe?

No. We show US Social Security 2025 figures separately from verified 2025 national top-ten ranks for England and Wales (ONS), France (INSEE), and Germany (GfdS). German ranks may group spellings. A name missing from a top-ten list may still be widely used.

Where are my saved names stored?

Your favorites are saved locally in your browser when storage is available. A share link contains only the selected names in its address. Anyone who receives the link can read those names. We do not store a shared shortlist on a server.

Sources & editorial method

US name counts: Social Security Administration, 2025 and 2024 national records, with a labeled 2020 fallback for the one preserved legacy name missing from 2025 records (US public data; the dataset suppresses names with fewer than five registrations per recorded sex). Etymology: short, source-derived glosses from English Wiktionary (CC BY-SA 4.0), assembled through the Lullabyte name-research dataset. Read the data notes after publication. The roots are linguistic and are not assertions of a name's exclusive national origin. 2025 European data: ONS, INSEE, and GfdS. Last checked for this release: November 2026.
